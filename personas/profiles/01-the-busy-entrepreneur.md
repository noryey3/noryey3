# P01 — The Busy Entrepreneur

> "I want a podcast. I just don't have time to run one."

---

## Who They Are

Solo founder, coach, or small business owner (1–10 people). Their podcast is a business tool — not a hobby. They see it as a lead generation and authority-building machine, but they're already running a business, serving clients, and doing 17 other things. The podcast is always the first thing that gets dropped when the week gets busy.

**Typical roles:** Founder, CEO, Solopreneur, Coach, Consultant, Online Business Owner
**Industries:** Coaching, Consulting, Real Estate, Finance, Marketing, E-commerce
**Budget:** $500–$2,500/mo

---

## Dream Outcome

**They don't want a podcast. They want what the podcast does for their business** — authority, leads, and content — WITHOUT it consuming their time.

The dream: they record once a week, their show goes out automatically, and it looks like they have a whole media team behind them. Their audience grows. Clients come in warmer. And they never once had to open GarageBand.

---

## Biggest Nightmare

Episodes pile up as unedited drafts. The show becomes inconsistent — two episodes in January, nothing in February, one in March. The audience they worked to build stops expecting new content. Their competitors with worse ideas but consistent publishing win the niche.

They feel guilty every week they don't publish. And they still have 6 hours of editing waiting for them.

---

## Winnability by Default: 8/10

This persona is HIGHLY winnable for cold outreach because:
- The pain is active and recurring (every week they don't publish)
- They have budget (they're running a business)
- They make their own decisions (solo or small team)
- The ROI is obvious once you frame it in their language (time = money)

---

## Green Lights (Raise Score to 9–10)

- Their last podcast episode was 4+ weeks ago (inconsistency = pain = urgency)
- They've posted anything about being "behind" or "inconsistent" or "overwhelmed"
- They're actively running a business — has a website, sells products/services
- They mention wanting to "batch" episodes or "get ahead" on content
- Their podcast has a business angle (not just a hobby topic)

## Red Flags (Lower Score or Skip)

- They post about podcasting as a hobby — no business model behind it
- Their business seems to be struggling (no recent activity, dead social presence)
- They've publicly said they enjoy the editing process — it's a feature, not a bug for them

---

## Cold Outreach Script

**Channel:** Email or LinkedIn DM
**Tone:** Direct, peer-to-peer, empathetic — not salesy

---

**Subject:** Your podcast episodes don't have to pile up

**Message:**

Hey [Name],

I've been listening to [Podcast Name] — [one specific thing you genuinely noticed: the topic, their format, a guest they had].

Quick question: how far behind are you on episodes right now?

I ask because most coaches/founders I talk to are in the same spot — they want to publish weekly, but between clients, content, and running the business, the podcast keeps getting deprioritized.

We handle everything after you hit record — editing, show notes, publishing to Spotify, Apple, everywhere. Most clients get their episode back in 48 hours.

Worth a 15-minute call to see if it makes sense for your show?

[Your name]

---

**Follow-up #1 (Day 3):**

> Hey [Name] — just circling back. Even if timing isn't right, happy to share what a fully hands-off podcast workflow looks like. Might be useful regardless.

**Follow-up #2 (Day 7):**

> Last touch — I know you're busy (that's literally the whole point). If you ever want to get your show running consistently without touching it yourself, I'm here. [Link to calendar]

---

## Discovery Call Questions

1. Walk me through your current editing process — what happens after you hit stop on the recording?
2. How many episodes are sitting in your drafts right now, waiting to be edited or published?
3. What would publishing consistently every week do for your business in the next 6 months?
4. What's the one piece of the podcast you most want to hand off permanently?
5. Have you tried outsourcing before? What happened?

---

## The Hormozi Reframe for This Persona

They will say: "It's expensive."
You say:

> "What's your hourly rate when you're working with clients? $200? $500? You're currently trading 4–5 of those hours every week to edit your podcast. That's $1,000 worth of your time going into a task that someone else can do for a fraction of that. The question isn't whether this is expensive — it's whether editing is the best use of your highest-dollar hours."
