# P02 — The DIY-er Who Hit the Wall

> "I used to be able to handle this myself. Now the business has grown and the old way doesn't work anymore."

---

## The Universal Archetype

They started small and did everything themselves — which was fine. But the business grew. What used to take an hour now takes a day. What used to fit in a spreadsheet now needs a system. What used to be "good enough" is now creating real problems — errors, gaps, delays, things slipping through the cracks. They know the old way is broken. They just haven't made the switch yet.

**The core pain:** They've outgrown their current approach but haven't upgraded yet.

**Typical signals:**
- Business is visibly growing (new hires, new locations, more content, more clients)
- Mentions of "needing to get organized," "clean things up," or "finally set up a proper system"
- They've been doing [the thing] themselves for 1–3 years — it's worked until now
- Increasing frequency of mistakes, delays, or things getting missed

**Demographics:** Business owners 1–3 years in, early-stage companies that have hit first growth, creators who scaled their audience and now need real infrastructure
**Budget:** $1,000–$3,000/mo
**Decision power:** Full if solo, partial if they have a co-founder or team

---

## Dream Outcome

A professional-grade setup that matches the level their business has reached. Clean systems, clean output, no more patching things together with spreadsheets and good intentions. They feel like a real company — because the back-end finally looks like one.

---

## Biggest Nightmare

They wait too long. The mess compounds. A tax issue surfaces. A client notices the inconsistency. A deal falls through because the financials aren't clean. Or they hand it off to someone and the transition is so painful that it sets them back weeks.

They procrastinate on upgrading because the cost of switching feels higher than the cost of staying broken — until it isn't.

---

## Winnability: 8/10 by default

The growth pressure means the problem is getting more urgent every month. They're already mentally sold on the idea of upgrading — they just need someone to make the transition feel painless. **Lower the switching cost and you close this lead.**

---

## Green Lights

- Business is clearly growing — more staff, more clients, more volume
- They mention their current system or process in a negative light ("our spreadsheet is a mess," "this doesn't scale")
- Recent business milestone: first hire, first $10K month, first investor conversation
- They've mentioned wanting to "get organized" or "set up proper systems"

## Red Flags

- Business growth has flatlined — they're in maintenance mode
- They're attached to the DIY approach as part of their identity ("I like being hands-on with this")
- They've attempted a switch before and it was so painful they went back to DIY

---

## Cold Outreach Script

**Tone:** Knowledgeable, confident. Speak like someone who has seen this growth stage before — because you have.

---

[Your name]

## Applied to Accounting Services

**Their exact pain:** They've been doing their books in Excel or QuickBooks themselves. The business doubled. Now the books are 3 months behind, records are a mess, and they're about to apply for a business loan and the bank wants audited financials — or they're staring down a big tax bill because no one was tracking estimated payments.

**Your hook:** "You've been managing your own books since you started. How's that holding up with where the business is now?"

**Your offer frame:** "We take over from where you are — no judgment on the current state — and get everything clean, reconciled, and IRS-compliant within 30 days. Then we handle it monthly going forward so you never fall behind again."

**Risk reversal:** "We'll do a free financial health check first — look at what you have, tell you exactly where the gaps are, and what it would take to fix it. No obligation."

---

## Discovery Call Questions

1. How are you currently handling your books — what does the setup look like?
2. When's the last time everything was fully reconciled and up to date?
3. What specifically has broken down about the current approach — what's the thing that's making you consider a change?
4. Are there any upcoming events that make clean financials urgent — loan, investor, audit, tax season?
5. What would a "properly organized" version of your finances look and feel like to you?
