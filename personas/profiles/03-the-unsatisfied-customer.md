# P03 — The Unsatisfied Customer

> "I'm already paying for this. I'm just not getting what I'm paying for."

---

## The Universal Archetype

They already have a solution — an accountant, a bookkeeper, an editor, an agency, a VA — but it's not working. The provider is slow, unresponsive, making mistakes, or just not growing with the business. They're staying because switching feels like more work than dealing with the frustration. But the frustration is mounting. They're one more missed deadline or ignored message away from being done.

**The core pain:** They're paying for a solution that isn't solving the problem.

**Typical signals:**
- Public complaints about their current provider (slow, unresponsive, errors)
- Mentions of switching, looking for someone new, or feeling "let down"
- Posts asking for referrals or recommendations for the service you provide
- Obvious quality issues with their output despite having paid help

**Demographics:** Any business size — this archetype is defined by situation, not stage. Could be a solo creator with a bad VA or a mid-size company with a bad accounting firm.
**Budget:** Already proven — they're currently paying for something. Switching cost is psychological, not financial.
**Decision power:** Usually full — they already made this decision once, they can make it again.

---

## Dream Outcome

A provider that just works. Deadlines are met without chasing. Quality is consistent. Communication is fast. They stop thinking about this as a problem — because it stops being one. They want the invisible, reliable partner they thought they were hiring the first time.

---

## Biggest Nightmare

They switch again and history repeats. New vendor, same problems. Now they've wasted time, money, and goodwill twice. The second bad experience is worse than the first because it confirms their fear: "maybe everyone in this space is like this."

Their biggest fear: **switching is just as bad as staying.**

---

## Winnability: 10/10 — Highest Priority

This is the hottest lead type in cold outreach. They are:
- Already buying the service (budget proven)
- Already unhappy (motivated to move)
- Already the decision-maker (they switched before)
- Actively looking (or about to be)

**The only thing you need to do is reduce the switching risk and be credible.**

---

## Green Lights

- Publicly asked for recommendations ("does anyone know a good accountant?")
- Complained about their current provider in a post or comment
- You can see obvious quality issues in their output despite having paid help (messy books, bad audio, inconsistent publishing)
- Their current vendor has bad reviews or known reputation issues
- They mentioned "switching," "looking for someone new," or being "let down"

## Red Flags

- They've switched 3+ times — might be an impossible-to-please client
- The complaint seems like a one-time vent, not a systemic problem (they may stick with their current provider)
- They're deeply loyal to a specific tool or process — the issue is the tool, not a vendor they'd replace

---

## Cold Outreach Script

**Tone:** Empathetic but confident. Don't bash their current provider — position around reliability and what you do differently.

---

**Subject:** Re: [finding a new accountant / looking for a new editor / your post about ___]

**Message:**

Hey [Name],

Saw your post about [looking for a new accountant / being let down by your current setup / needing a recommendation] — completely understand the frustration.

We work with business owners who've had that same experience: paying for a service and not getting what they expected. Most of my clients came from exactly that situation.

What we do differently: [one specific thing — monthly reports delivered by the 10th, 48-hour turnaround, direct access to your accountant, whatever your differentiator is]. That's the thing that usually matters most after someone's been burned.

Worth a 15-minute call? Happy to walk through what our process looks like so you can decide if it's different enough to be worth trying.

[Your name]

---

**Follow-up #1 (Day 2 — faster than usual, they're actively looking):**

> Hey [Name] — following up quickly since I know you're actively looking. Happy to share some client examples this week if that helps speed up the decision. What's your timeline for making a switch?

**Follow-up #2 (Day 5):**

> Last one from me. If you've already found someone, hope it's a great fit. If not and you're still evaluating, here's my calendar: [link]. We can usually onboard within a week.

---

## Applied to Accounting Services

**Their exact pain:** Their current CPA only contacts them at tax season. Books are never updated. They have no idea how the business is actually doing financially. Or: their bookkeeper keeps making errors and they spend hours correcting reconciliations.

**Your hook:** "I saw you're looking for a new accountant — what's been the main issue with your current setup?"

**Your offer frame:** "We do monthly closes — every month by the 10th, you get a full P&L, balance sheet, and cash flow report. Your books are always current. You always know your numbers. And if you have a question, we respond same day."

**Risk reversal:** "We'll take your last 3 months of books and show you what our output looks like before you commit. If it's not clearly better than what you have, don't switch."

---

## Discovery Call Questions

1. What specifically hasn't been working with your current setup — is it quality, communication, turnaround, or all of the above?
2. How long have you been dealing with this issue?
3. What does "working" look like to you — what would an ideal provider do differently?
4. What's your timeline for making a switch?
5. What made you stay this long — what would have to change for the new relationship to stick?
