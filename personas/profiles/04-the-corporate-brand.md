# P04 — The Corporate Brand

> "Our company needs a podcast that sounds like a market leader. Not like someone's hobby project."

---

## Who They Are

A business, agency, or organization launching or maintaining a branded podcast as part of their marketing, thought leadership, or recruitment strategy. The contact you're reaching is usually a Marketing Director, Head of Content, or CMO — not the final decision-maker. Multiple stakeholders are involved. The brand is everything. Professional standards are non-negotiable.

**Typical roles:** Marketing Director, Head of Content, CMO, Communications Manager, Founder (50+ employee company)
**Industries:** Finance, Legal, Healthcare, SaaS, Technology, Professional Services, Recruitment
**Budget:** $2,500–$5,000+/mo

---

## Dream Outcome

Their podcast becomes the #1 resource in their industry. Clients and prospects say "I listen to your podcast." The show generates inbound interest — partnerships, press, speaking invitations, warm leads arriving pre-sold. Leadership sees it as a core marketing asset, not an experiment.

Internally: the content team is no longer stressed about production. Every episode ships on time, sounds impeccable, and goes through their brand approval process smoothly.

---

## Biggest Nightmare

They launch the podcast, spend money on it, and it sounds like it was recorded in a closet with a $30 microphone. Or worse — they hire someone, the first few episodes are great, and then production quality quietly degrades. Leadership starts asking questions. The budget gets cut.

Or: they can't get episodes out on time because the production partner doesn't understand their internal approval process, and suddenly the show is a source of internal friction instead of pride.

---

## Winnability by Default: 6/10

Good money, longer sales cycle. The budget is there but the decision is committee-driven and slow. Don't expect to close in one call. Your job in cold outreach is to GET THE MEETING — not to close. The close happens over 2–4 conversations. Patience and professionalism are the competitive advantage here.

---

## Green Lights (Raise Score to 8–10)

- They've announced a podcast recently or posted a job listing for a podcast producer
- Their competitors have branded podcasts — competitive pressure is a strong motivator
- They're hosting a conference, launching a product, or going through a major company moment (rebrand, Series A, expansion)
- Their marketing team is active and growing — content investment is clearly happening
- They have a large existing audience (newsletter, social, conference following)

## Red Flags (Lower Score or Skip)

- The company is in financial difficulty or has announced layoffs
- The contact you found has no marketing authority — they can't champion the project internally
- They've attempted a podcast before and it failed and was abandoned
- The company is highly regulated (pharmaceuticals, securities) and the compliance burden is prohibitive

---

## Cold Outreach Script

**Channel:** LinkedIn DM or professional email — NOT Instagram, NOT Twitter DMs
**Tone:** Professional, structured, confident. No slang. No casualness. Be the vendor they trust before they've talked to you.

---

**Subject:** Branded podcast production for [Company Name]

**Email:**

Hi [Name],

I came across [Company Name] — [specific observation: their industry, a recent announcement, their content marketing effort].

Quick question: does [Company Name] have a branded podcast, or is that something in the roadmap?

I work with companies in [their industry] producing branded podcasts for thought leadership and client engagement — full production, end-to-end, aligned with brand standards and internal approval workflows.

If it's something you're exploring, happy to share what a fully managed podcast looks like at the corporate level. 30 minutes — no pitch, just context.

Best,
[Your name]

---

**Follow-up #1 (Day 5, LinkedIn):**

> Hi [Name] — just following up on my email. Podcasting is moving faster in [their industry] than most companies expect — [Competitor] launched theirs in Q4 and it's already outperforming their blog traffic. Happy to walk through what a professional branded production setup looks like for a company at your stage.

**Follow-up #2 (Day 12, email):**

> Hi [Name] — last follow-up. I know the inbox is busy. If a branded podcast is something [Company Name] has on the roadmap for this year, I'd love 20 minutes to walk you through what other [industry] companies are doing with it. Otherwise, no worries — I'll respect your inbox. [Calendar link]

---

## Discovery Call Questions

1. What's the business objective behind the podcast — thought leadership, recruiting, client retention, or a mix?
2. Who are the key stakeholders involved in this decision, and what does the approval process look like?
3. Have you worked with external production vendors before? What worked, what didn't?
4. What does brand standards compliance look like for content — legal review, brand sign-off, executive approval?
5. What would success look like at the 6-month mark — what metric would justify continuing the investment to leadership?
6. Is there a launch date or company event you're tying the podcast to?

---

## The Hormozi Reframe for This Persona

They will say: "We need to get budget approval — this isn't something I can decide alone."
You say:

> "Totally understood. Would it help if I put together a one-page ROI summary you can bring to that conversation? I can outline expected reach, content repurposing value, and how other [industry] companies are using their branded podcasts to measure impact. That way you walk into that meeting with data, not just a proposal. Want me to send that over?"

Then you've moved from "vendor trying to close a deal" to "partner helping them win internally." That's what gets corporate deals done.
