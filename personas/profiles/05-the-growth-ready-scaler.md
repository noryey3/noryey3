# P05 — The Growth-Ready Scaler

> "We're about to grow — and I need the foundation to actually support it."

---

## The Universal Archetype

A business owner preparing for a significant next step — a funding round, a new market, a franchise expansion, a key hire, or a major partnership. They understand that their current setup won't survive the growth they're planning. They need to professionalize BEFORE the growth happens, not scramble to catch up after. They're proactive, forward-looking, and willing to invest in infrastructure because they see it as a prerequisite to the goal.

**The core pain:** They can see where they're going — and they know their current back-end can't get them there.

**Typical signals:**
- Announced a new product, market expansion, or major company milestone
- Actively raising funding or preparing for investor conversations
- Hiring fast — team is growing from 2 to 10+
- Building toward a franchise, acquisition, or IPO
- Big client or contract coming in that will expose any weaknesses in their current setup

**Demographics:** Businesses 2–5 years in, at a clear inflection point. Usually have existing revenue ($500K–$5M+). Team is small but growing.
**Budget:** $2,500–$10,000+/mo — they're investment-minded. They see the service as infrastructure, not overhead.
**Decision power:** Usually the founder or CEO — high authority, but may consult a CFO, COO, or board.

---

## Dream Outcome

They go into their next growth phase with everything already set up: clean books, a real financial system, professional-grade processes, and a back-office that can handle 10x the current volume without breaking. Their investors, bank, or partners look at the operation and see a company that knows what it's doing.

The deeper dream: they stop being an entrepreneur running a scrappy operation and start feeling like a CEO running a real company.

---

## Biggest Nightmare

They close the funding round, land the big client, or hire the team — and then the back-office falls apart under the pressure. The books aren't clean enough for due diligence. The financial reports don't exist or can't be trusted. A major opportunity is lost or delayed because the infrastructure wasn't ready.

Growing into chaos is worse than staying small.

---

## Winnability: 7/10

High-value leads but requires a more strategic conversation. They're not buying a commodity — they're buying a partner for a critical growth phase. The sale is about confidence and capability. Show them you've done this before at their scale. **Be the person who's been to where they're going.**

---

## Green Lights

- Just announced a funding round, major hire, or expansion plan
- Preparing for due diligence (M&A, investment, partnership)
- Revenue is clearly accelerating — more staff, more clients, more locations
- Posting about "building the right team," "getting systems in place," or "professionalizing operations"
- They have a specific deadline — "we're closing the round in Q2," "we're opening the second location in March"

## Red Flags

- Growth is aspirational but not imminent — they've been "about to scale" for 2 years
- They see back-office as overhead, not infrastructure — they'll undervalue what you offer
- The growth event is so far out that the urgency isn't there yet

---

## Cold Outreach Script

**Tone:** Peer-level, strategic, forward-looking. This is not a budget conversation. This is a vision conversation. Speak to where they're going, not where they are.

---

**Subject:** [Company Name]'s next stage — is the back-office ready?

**Message:**

Hey [Name],

Saw [the announcement / the LinkedIn post / the news] about [specific growth milestone — funding, expansion, new hire, new market] — congrats, that's a big move.

Quick question from someone who works with companies at this stage: is your [accounting / financial reporting / operations] setup ready to scale with it?

The thing that usually breaks first when a business moves from [current stage] to [next stage] is the back-office — books that were fine at $X revenue start creating real problems at $2X, especially for due diligence, payroll, or investor reporting.

I work with founders going through exactly this transition. Happy to do a 20-minute call to look at where your current setup stands and whether it needs to be upgraded before the growth hits.

[Your name]

---

**Follow-up #1 (Day 5):**

> Hey [Name] — following up. The timing on this matters — building the financial infrastructure AFTER the growth event is always more painful than before. If you want to walk through what your books and reporting need to look like for [the next stage], I'm happy to do that this week.

**Follow-up #2 (Day 10):**

> Last message. If the [funding / expansion / team growth] is still on the roadmap, and you want a back-office that can handle it cleanly, I'm here. [Calendar link]

## Applied to Accounting Services

**Their exact pain:** They're about to close a Series A and their books are in QuickBooks but have never been properly reviewed. Or they're applying for a bank loan and the bank wants 2 years of audited financials. Or they just hired their 10th employee and suddenly payroll is a real thing with real compliance requirements. Or they're negotiating a franchise agreement and the franchisor needs to see clean financial records.

**Your hook:** "Congrats on [the milestone] — is your financial reporting set up to support due diligence if it comes to that?"

**Your offer frame:** "We set up and manage a full accounting system — monthly closes, consolidated financial statements, payroll management, BIR compliance, and investor-ready reporting. When your bank, investor, or partner asks to see your numbers, everything is ready and clean."

**The strategic reframe:** "The cost of getting this right now is a fraction of what it costs to clean it up under pressure during due diligence. Every week of messy books before that conversation is a week of risk that could slow down or kill the deal."

**Risk reversal:** "We'll do a free financial readiness assessment — look at your current books and reporting, tell you exactly what a bank or investor would flag, and what it would take to fix it before that conversation happens."

---

## Discovery Call Questions

1. Tell me about the growth milestone — what's the timeline and what does success look like?
2. What does your current financial reporting setup look like — who handles the books, how current are they, what reports do you have access to?
3. Are there any upcoming conversations — with investors, a bank, a partner — that will require you to show your financials?
4. What does your current team look like on the finance and ops side?
5. If an investor asked to see your P&L and balance sheet today, how confident are you that what you'd show them is accurate and clean?
