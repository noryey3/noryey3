# P02 — The Established Podcaster

> "I've built a real show. I just need production that matches what I've built."

---

## Who They Are

A serious podcaster with an existing show, a real audience, and a track record of consistent publishing. They've put in the work — 50, 100, 200+ episodes. The DIY setup they used to get here is now a ceiling. Either quality has plateaued, turnaround is unreliable, or their current editor keeps flaking. They're not a beginner. They need a PARTNER, not a vendor.

**Typical roles:** Podcaster, Author, Speaker, Industry Expert, Thought Leader, Educator
**Industries:** Media, Business, Finance, Health, Tech, True Crime, Comedy
**Budget:** $1,000–$2,500/mo

---

## Dream Outcome

They want their show to become THE reference podcast in their niche. Their audio sounds like a Spotify Original. Show notes rank on Google. Guests are pre-researched and perfectly matched. Every episode goes out on time, every time — without them worrying about it.

The secondary dream: their show attracts sponsorships, speaking gigs, and business opportunities automatically. It works FOR them, not the other way around.

---

## Biggest Nightmare

They hand off their show to someone, the first few episodes are fine, then quality starts to slip — the editor stops responding on time, small things get missed, and they're now doing QA on top of everything else. Or worse: their editor disappears and they're scrambling to publish while managing everything else.

They've built something. They can't afford for someone to mess it up.

---

## Winnability by Default: 7/10

Winnable but requires more trust-building than P01. The pain is real but the risk of switching is also real — they have an existing workflow, even if it's imperfect. The key is credibility: show them you understand their show, you have a documented process, and your reliability is provable.

---

## Green Lights (Raise Score to 9–10)

- Their recent episodes have obvious audio quality issues (background noise, inconsistent levels, no music)
- They post about their editor "flaking" or content being "late"
- Episodes are getting less frequent — the publishing schedule is slipping
- They're actively growing (new guests, new audience segments, monetization)
- They've mentioned wanting to add content research, show notes, or SEO to their process

## Red Flags (Lower Score or Skip)

- They seem deeply loyal to their current editor (posts appreciation content for them)
- Their production quality is already excellent — you'd need to offer something beyond editing
- They're in maintenance mode and not actively trying to grow

---

## Cold Outreach Script

**Channel:** Email or Twitter/X DM — wherever they're most active
**Tone:** Peer-to-peer. Respect the work they've done. Don't condescend.

---

**Subject:** [Podcast Name] — a quick production question

**Message:**

Hey [Name],

Been listening to [Podcast Name] — specifically [episode title or topic]. Really liked [specific genuine observation — a guest they had, a segment, a take].

I work with established podcasters on production — editing, show notes, content research — the behind-the-scenes side. Most of my clients come to me when their current setup is getting inconsistent or quality has plateaued and they want to take the show to the next tier.

Quick question: how's your current production workflow holding up? Specifically turnaround time and consistency?

If it's working great, awesome. If not, worth a 20-minute conversation. Happy to share what we do differently.

[Your name]

---

**Follow-up #1 (Day 4):**

> Hey [Name] — just following up. If your current editor is solid and you're happy with turnaround, no need to change anything. But if consistency or quality has become a recurring issue, that's usually a sign the current setup has hit its ceiling. Happy to talk.

**Follow-up #2 (Day 10):**

> Last message, promise. I know you're busy building the show. If you ever want a second set of ears on your production workflow or want to explore what a production upgrade looks like, I'm here. [Calendar link]

---

## Discovery Call Questions

1. Tell me about your current production setup — who edits, what's the turnaround, where does it break down?
2. What's your biggest frustration with the current process?
3. How important is turnaround time — what's your record-to-publish window right now?
4. Are you happy with how your show notes are driving discovery? Where do you think you're leaving SEO on the table?
5. Where do you want the show to be in 12 months — more listeners, sponsorships, a second show?

---

## The Hormozi Reframe for This Persona

They will say: "I'm worried about losing my show's voice if I switch editors."
You say:

> "That's the right thing to worry about. Here's how we handle it: the first two episodes are a calibration period. You give us your style notes — what you love, what you hate, specific edits you always make — and we match it exactly. If episode two doesn't sound exactly like YOUR show, we redo it. Most clients tell us episode two sounds better than anything they'd done before, because we're applying their preferences consistently across every episode, not just some of them."
