# How to Customize This System for Any Service

This persona generator is built around **buyer situations**, not specific industries.

The same five archetypes exist whether you're selling accounting services, podcast production, legal services, marketing, IT support, or cleaning services. The situations don't change — only the specific pain, hook, and offer do.

Use this guide to adapt the system to any client or service in under 30 minutes.

---

## Step 1 — Define the Service in One Sentence

Fill this in before anything else:

```
Service: ________________________________
Provider: ________________________________
Target: _________________________________ (what kind of business or person)
Core promise: ___________________________ (what outcome do you deliver?)
Timeframe: ______________________________ (how fast do they see it?)
What they do: ___________________________ (minimal effort required from client)
What you do: ____________________________ (everything else)
```

**Accounting example:**
```
Service: Monthly bookkeeping + federal/state tax compliance
Provider: [Accounting firm name]
Target: Small business owners and SMEs in the US
Core promise: Clean books, zero missed filings, full financial visibility
Timeframe: Full setup in 2 weeks, monthly reports by the 10th
What they do: Send bank statements and receipts
What you do: Everything else — reconciliations, quarterly estimates, filings, payroll, 1099s
```

## Step 3 — Define Your Risk Reversals

A risk reversal removes the biggest reason someone doesn't buy. For each service, choose at least one:

| Option | Best for |
|--------|---------|
| First month free | Subscription services where you're confident in retention |
| Free audit / assessment | Services where showing the problem creates urgency (accounting, IT, legal) |
| First deliverable free | Project-based services — let the work prove itself |
| Money-back guarantee | When quality is highly subjective |
| No contract — cancel anytime | When the switching cost from a competitor is psychological |
| Pilot project at reduced rate | High-ticket services where trust needs to be built first |

## Step 4 — Define the Winnability Signals for Your Service

The winnability framework criteria are universal. But the specific signals that trigger each criterion are service-specific. Fill these in:

```
PROBLEM AWARENESS signals for [your service]:
- _______________________________________________
- _______________________________________________
- _______________________________________________

BUDGET signals for [your service / target market]:
- _______________________________________________
- _______________________________________________

TIMING signals for [your service]:
- _______________________________________________
- _______________________________________________
```

**Accounting example:**
```
PROBLEM AWARENESS:
- Posted about IRS notices, tax stress, or missed estimated payments
- Behind on quarterly estimated taxes or payroll filings
- Mentioned doing books themselves in Excel or QuickBooks
- Recently hired employees (new payroll tax and W-2 burden)
- Paid contractors and unsure whether 1099s are required

BUDGET:
- Has a registered business with visible revenue
- Sells products or services online
- Has staff or contractors (means money is moving)

TIMING:
- Quarterly estimated tax deadline approaching (Jan 15, Apr 15, Jun 15, Sep 15)
- Just registered a new business or hired their first employee
- Tax season (Jan–April) — when pain is highest
- Announcing growth (new hire, new product, expansion)
- Responding to an IRS or state tax notice
```
